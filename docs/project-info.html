MyVideoEditor is a free video editor, converter, AI upscaler and screen recorder for Windows and Linux. Everything runs on your own computer: no account, no subscription, no watermark and no uploads. It's open source under the MIT licence.

Download: Windows, from the Microsoft Store · Linux, from GitHub

What it does

Edit

  • Multi-track timeline for video, sound and pictures, with picture-in-picture and overlays
  • Move, trim, split, ripple-delete and undo anything
  • Transitions: dissolves, wipes, pushes, covers, circle, clock, zoom, pixelate and blur
  • Titles, lower thirds, captions and end cards, with animations, credits rolls, an opening crawl and a news ticker
  • Colour correction, stabilisation, green screen, noise reduction and sound effects
  • Speed from 0.1× to 16× with the pitch kept; autosave with crash recovery

Export

  • MP4 (H.264, H.265, AV1), WebM, MKV, MOV (ProRes) and animated GIF
  • Audio only: MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC and Opus
  • Any size, from vertical Shorts and Reels to 4K
  • Chapters, and DVD-Video (NTSC or PAL) ready to burn
  • The preview uses the same engine as the export, so what you see is what you get

Convert and upscale

  • Batch conversion: change format, resize, trim, rotate, deinterlace or change the frame rate
  • Rewrap into a new container without re-encoding
  • Upscale with built-in AI models (Real-ESRGAN, Real-CUGAN, ArtCNN and SmallSR), on the graphics card or the processor
  • Compare every method on a frame of your own video before a long job
  • Your originals are never changed

Record and annotate

  • Record a window, a screen or a region, with the microphone and computer sound
  • Take screenshots of the same targets
  • Add arrows, boxes, callouts, numbered steps and spotlights
  • Blur or pixelate passwords and personal details
  • Export a small MP4 or GIF for a bug report, or a PNG

Upscaling for every kind of PC

Old or low-resolution video can be made larger with methods that range from fast classic filters to AI models. All of them are free and run locally. Test speed on this PC times each method on a few frames of your own video and recommends the best one that finishes in reasonable time. Compare all methods shows one frame through every method side by side, with shared zoom and pan. A strength control mixes the AI result with a faithful enlargement, so faces and textures stay natural.

On Windows the AI models can use any DirectX 12 graphics card. A thread limit and a low-priority setting keep laptops cool and responsive during long jobs.

Private by design

MyVideoEditor collects nothing and has no account. Your videos never leave your computer, and the AI models run locally. The app only goes online to download an optional upscaling component, and only when you ask for one. Recording happens only when you start it. Read the privacy policy.

Download and install

Windows

Install MyVideoEditor from the Microsoft Store. It installs and updates automatically. It needs Windows 10 version 1809 or later, x64.

Linux

Download myvideoeditor-<version>-linux-x64.tar.gz from the latest release on GitHub, extract it and run ./install.sh. It installs for your user under ~/.local and adds MyVideoEditor to your applications menu. It needs a 64-bit (x64) system. Screen recording uses X11, so in a Wayland session only XWayland apps can be captured.

FFmpeg is included on both platforms, so there's nothing else to install.

Open source

MyVideoEditor is written in C# on .NET 10 with Avalonia, and uses FFmpeg for decoding and encoding. The source code, issue tracker and releases are on GitHub. It's released under the MIT licence; bundled components keep their own licences, listed in the app under Help → Licences.
